Wind speed has the strongest link to signal strength

365 days of data, MTN and Airtel, Port Harcourt, 2025.

Wind speed explains more of the drop in signal than humidity, temperature or pressure.

Signal strength by wind speed

Average signal strength (dBm) for days grouped into six wind-speed bands. Higher on the chart means a stronger signal. The top band has only 19 days, so it is the least reliable point.

Regression model

A linear regression using all four weather variables explains about 53% of the day-to-day variation in signal strength (R² = 0.53) for each network. The other 47% comes from things not in this dataset, such as congestion, tower distance and terrain.

Data source

The data was measured, not simulated. It comes from a custom weather and GSM monitoring station built for the PhD research this analysis supports. The station records wind, temperature, humidity and pressure alongside MTN and Airtel signal strength. The readings are the client's, and I analysed them without collecting them myself.

Limitations

  • The data covers one city and one year.
  • Each reading is a daily value, so changes within a day are not captured.
  • The model is linear. It cannot pick up effects that are not a straight line.
  • Correlation does not show cause. Wind may be standing in for something else, such as storms that bring heavy load on the network.
  • Signal strength is affected by things the station did not record, such as congestion, tower distance and terrain.
  • I checked for outliers using the IQR rule. The flagged days were mostly low-wind and low-humidity days in the dry season, and a few high-wind days. I kept them because they appear to be real readings. Removing them lowered the wind correlation slightly (MTN -0.61 to -0.57, Airtel -0.62 to -0.54) but did not change the conclusion.
